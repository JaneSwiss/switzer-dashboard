Three years of running my own Pinterest account taught me something the tutorials don't mention.

Most accounts don't fail because they're not posting enough.

They fail because they're built wrong from the start.

Here's what that actually looks like:

**Your board names are search terms.** Pinterest is a search engine — people type "branding kit for coaches" or "website templates for therapists" and then find pins. If your boards are named "My designs" or "Inspiration" instead of something people actually search for, the algorithm has nowhere to put you. You're invisible before anyone even sees your pins.

**Pinning in batches kills your reach.** 20 pins on Monday, nothing until next week — Pinterest reads that as inconsistency and starts showing your content to fewer people. 3-5 pins spread through the day, consistently, is what actually moves the dial.

**The first image has three seconds.** If someone can't tell what your pin is about at a glance — text too small, image too busy, no clear message — they scroll past. Pinterest tracks those scroll-bys and quietly reduces how often your pins get shown.

Three things. All fixable.
