SLIDE 1: Most free Instagram templates create a different problem. They're either so generic your feed looks like everyone else's — or so complicated that editing them takes forever.

SLIDE 2: The biggest red flag? Tiny text boxes. If you can't fit a real sentence without shrinking the font to unreadable, skip it. Templates should work with your content, not fight it.

SLIDE 3: Look for simple color blocks you can swap easily. Most free templates are built around the designer's palette. If changing colors breaks the whole design, it's not saving you time.

SLIDE 4: Your eye should know exactly where to look first. Good templates have clear visual hierarchy. If everything competes for attention, the design isn't doing its job.

SLIDE 5: Skip the first few pages in Canva. The most popular templates are overexposed. Search for your niche instead — like "coach Instagram template" or "consultant post."

SLIDE 6: A useful template gives you a reliable structure you can customize quickly without design skills. Pretty is nice. Practical is better.

You've spent an hour on a "free" template that looked perfect in the preview.

The truth is most free template libraries care about volume, not quality. They need thousands of options to look impressive — so they churn out designs that technically work but don't help your business stand out.
