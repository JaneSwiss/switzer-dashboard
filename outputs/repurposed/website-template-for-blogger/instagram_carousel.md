COVER: The prettiest template is rarely the best one

SLIDE 1: That template demo looks perfect because it was styled with curated photos, short placeholder text, and a designer's eye. Your actual blog won't look like that on day one. Maybe ever.

SLIDE 2: Over 60% of blog traffic comes from phones. If your template doesn't look good on mobile, you're losing readers before they finish your first paragraph. Check the mobile preview before you commit.

SLIDE 3: A gorgeous template that loads slowly is actively hurting you. If the demo takes more than three seconds to load on your phone, walk away. Google penalizes slow sites and readers bounce fast.

SLIDE 4: Your readers should know exactly where to go within two seconds of landing on your site. Confusing menus and hidden categories create friction. Simple navigation beats clever navigation every time.

SLIDE 5: Free templates can be excellent — but many come with bloated code, limited customization, and zero support when something breaks. The cost of fixing a bad template often exceeds what you'd have spent on a quality paid option.

SLIDE 6: Before you browse another gallery: get clear on what your blog actually needs. A lifestyle blogger and a business coach need completely different things from their template. Match the template to your purpose, not just your Pinterest board.

CAPTION:
I've watched so many business owners spend hours scrolling template galleries, only to pick something that looks pretty but doesn't actually work for their content.

Here's the thing — that demo was designed by professionals with perfect placeholder content. Your real photos, your actual post titles, your logo? They need a template that adapts to you.

What matters more than aesthetics: mobile responsiveness, loading speed, clear navigation, and easy customization. These affect whether people stay on your site or bounce before reading a single word.
