COVER: That template is tanking your sales

SLIDE 1: Every Shopify demo store looks stunning. Perfect photography. Curated collections. Professional styling. But your store has real products with real variants — and that's a completely different situation.

SLIDE 2: A template built for 500 SKUs will make your 20-item collection look sparse and incomplete. A template built for minimal products will break when you add size and colour variants. Match your catalog size first.

SLIDE 3: Over 70% of your customers are shopping on mobile. They have maybe 90 seconds before their meeting starts or their kid interrupts. If your product page doesn't load fast and look right on a phone, they're gone.

SLIDE 4: Before you open a single template preview, write down three things: your product count, your variant complexity (sizes, colours, bundles), and whether your products need storytelling space or rapid checkout.

SLIDE 5: Image-heavy products like jewellery and home goods need large photography sections. Variant-heavy products like clothing need clear swatches and size guides that actually work on mobile. Different products, different needs.

SLIDE 6: The right template matches your catalog, loads fast on mobile, and includes features your product type needs — without forcing you to buy ten apps to fill the gaps. That single decision shapes everything.

CAPTION:
Most sellers choose a template because the demo looked beautiful.

Then they spend six months fighting against features designed for a completely different business model. A candle maker doesn't need the same checkout flow as a print-on-demand t-shirt store. A curated baby boutique doesn't need the same navigation as a dropshipping operation.

Your template becomes the architecture your entire store runs on. It affects whether visitors trust you enough to buy, whether Google ranks you, and whether your store feels like a real business.
