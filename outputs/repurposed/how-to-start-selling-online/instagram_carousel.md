COVER: Sell before you're ready.

SLIDE 1: Your first customers won't be strangers. They'll be friends, family, that person from your old job who likes everything you post. This isn't embarrassing — it's how you learn what actually works.

SLIDE 2: Before you pick a platform, your product needs to pass three tests. Can you ship it affordably? Can you price it at 3-4x your cost? Are people already searching for it?

SLIDE 3: No competition usually means no demand. Search your product idea on Etsy. Dozens of sellers with thousands of sales? Good news — buyers exist.

SLIDE 4: If it costs you $8 to make and ship, you need to sell it for $24-32. Anything less and you're working for free after marketing costs.

SLIDE 5: You don't need a website to start. Marketplaces like Etsy bring you customers. Your own site means you bring yourself customers. Start where the buyers already are.

SLIDE 6: The businesses that succeed online aren't the ones with the best branding. They're the ones that started selling before they felt ready.

CAPTION:
I've watched so many people spend months getting ready to sell online — and never actually sell anything.

The custom website. The perfect logo. The LLC paperwork. Meanwhile, their products sit in boxes.

Here's the thing: your first 10 sales will come from people who already know you. That's not a problem to solve. That's your foundation to build on.

Those early sales teach you how shipping actually works. They get you reviews. They show you what questions people ask before they buy.

Only after that does chasing strangers make sense.
