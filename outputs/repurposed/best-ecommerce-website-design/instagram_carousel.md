COVER: Pretty stores don't sell. Clear ones do.

SLIDE 1: Your homepage has one job. Show people what you sell and make it obvious how to buy it. Not your origin story. Not your Instagram feed. Products first.

SLIDE 2: Open your store on your phone right now. Screenshot what appears before you scroll. If there's no product or path to products visible — you're losing sales.

SLIDE 3: Product photos matter more than layout. No amount of beautiful design compensates for inconsistent images. Show multiple angles. Show scale. Show it in use.

SLIDE 4: Trust signals belong near the buy button. Free shipping thresholds, return policy snippets, secure checkout badges. Not buried in your FAQ or footer.

SLIDE 5: Guest checkout is non-negotiable. Forcing account creation before purchase is the fastest way to lose a sale. One toggle in Shopify. Fix it today.

SLIDE 6: The stores making consistent sales aren't doing anything fancy. They're just not making buying feel like work.

CAPTION:
I've seen dozens of Shopify stores fail — not because the products were bad, but because the website made buying feel like work.

Customers landed, got confused, and left. The owner blamed marketing. The problem was design.

The difference between stores that convert and stores that don't usually comes down to clarity, not aesthetics. Can someone tell what you sell in three seconds? Find what they want in two clicks? Check out without jumping through hoops?

If you're getting traffic but not sales, the issue probably isn't your products. It's friction you don't even know is there.
