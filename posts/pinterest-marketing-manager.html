WHAT A PINTEREST MARKETING MANAGER ACTUALLY DOES (DAY TO DAY)

The title sounds straightforward, but what happens behind the scenes is more involved than most people realize. A Pinterest marketing manager isn't just scheduling pins. They're building a system that compounds over time.

Keyword research forms the foundation of everything. Before a single pin gets created, Jane researches what your ideal clients are actually typing into Pinterest search. Not what you think they're searching for - what the data shows they're searching for. This includes primary keywords, long-tail variations, and trending terms in your niche. The difference between a pin that gets buried and one that drives traffic for months often comes down to this research.

Board strategy determines how Pinterest categorizes your content. Each board needs a keyword-optimized title, a detailed description, and a clear topic focus. Random boards with clever names don't get found. Strategic boards with searchable names do.

Pin design and creation is where the visual work happens. Every pin needs to stop the scroll, communicate value instantly, and include text that reinforces the keyword strategy. Jane creates multiple pin designs for your key content - because one piece of content deserves several chances to be found.

Scheduling and publishing keeps your account active without you logging in daily. Pins are scheduled at optimal times based on when your audience is actually online. Consistency matters more than volume - a steady stream of quality pins beats sporadic bursts of activity.

Monthly analytics review tells you what's working and what needs adjustment. Not vanity metrics like impressions, but actionable data: which pins drive the most outbound clicks, which keywords perform best, where your traffic actually comes from. This is how strategy evolves instead of staying static.

WHAT TO REALISTICALLY EXPECT IN YOUR FIRST 30-60-90 DAYS

Pinterest is a long game. Anyone promising immediate viral results is either lying or doesn't understand how the platform works. Here's what actually happens when you invest in proper Pinterest marketing.

Days 1-30: Foundation phase. Your account gets audited and optimized. Boards get restructured with keyword-rich titles and descriptions. Your profile gets updated to clearly communicate who you help. First pins get created and scheduled. During this phase, you might not see dramatic traffic increases - and that's normal. Pinterest is indexing your content, understanding what your account is about, and deciding where to show your pins in search results.

Days 31-60: Traction phase. Pins from the first month start gaining impressions and saves. You'll see which keywords resonate with your audience. Analytics begin showing patterns - certain pin styles outperform others, specific topics generate more clicks, particular times see higher engagement. This data informs the next round of content creation.

Days 61-90: Momentum phase. Your best-performing pins continue driving traffic months after posting. New pins benefit from your account's established authority in your niche. Traffic from Pinterest starts showing up consistently in your website analytics. If you're tracking email signups or inquiries, you'll notice leads coming from Pinterest who found you through search - not because they were already following you.

The compounding effect is the whole point. A pin you create in month one can drive traffic in month six, month twelve, and beyond. This is fundamentally different from Instagram or Facebook, where content dies within days. The patience required upfront pays off in sustainable traffic that doesn't disappear when you stop posting.

THE MOST COMMON MISTAKES BUSINESS OWNERS MAKE WITH PINTEREST

Understanding what doesn't work is as valuable as knowing what does. These mistakes waste months of effort.

Treating Pinterest like Instagram. Pinterest rewards different behavior. Hashtags barely matter. Follower count is secondary to search visibility. Posting multiple times daily isn't necessary. The strategies that work on Instagram often actively hurt you on Pinterest because you're optimizing for the wrong metrics.

Using pretty pins with no keyword strategy. Beautiful pins that don't match what people search for don't get found. Pinterest isn't a portfolio - it's a search engine. A strategically titled pin with decent design will outperform a stunning pin with a clever-but-unsearchable title every time.

Expecting overnight results then quitting. Most business owners try Pinterest for 2-3 weeks, see minimal traction, and assume it doesn't work for their niche. Pinterest typically takes 3-6 months to show meaningful results. The people who succeed are the ones who understand this timeline going in.

Pinning inconsistently. Sporadic bursts of activity followed by weeks of silence confuse the algorithm. Pinterest wants to see regular, steady activity. Five pins per week every week beats 35 pins one week and nothing for the next month.

Ignoring analytics. If you're not looking at which pins drive outbound clicks (not just impressions or saves), you can't improve your strategy. Saves are nice. Clicks to your website are what actually matter for your business.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Is hiring a Pinterest marketing manager actually worth it?

It depends on what your time is worth and whether you'll realistically do the work yourself. If you've tried managing Pinterest and couldn't stay consistent, or if your hourly rate exceeds what you'd pay someone to manage it properly, hiring makes financial sense. The ROI shows up in traffic that compounds over months - not in the first week.

How much do Pinterest marketing managers typically charge?

Rates vary widely based on scope and experience. Strategy-only packages range from $300-800 as a one-time deliverable. Ongoing monthly management ranges from $400-1,500 depending on pin volume and additional services. Jane's packages at pinterest.switzertemplates.com start at $699 for strategy and $1,299 for full setup - both one-time investments rather than monthly retainers.

What's the difference between a Pinterest manager and a Pinterest virtual assistant?

A Pinterest virtual assistant typically handles execution - scheduling pins you've created, following a strategy you've developed. A Pinterest marketing manager handles strategy and execution - keyword research, content planning, pin design, and analytics interpretation. The manager role includes strategic thinking, not just task completion.

How long before I see results from Pinterest marketing?

Expect 30-60 days before pins start gaining meaningful traction, and 90 days before you can accurately evaluate whether your strategy is working. Pinterest indexes content gradually, and the compounding effect that makes it valuable requires patience. Anyone promising faster results doesn't understand the platform.

Can I just do Pinterest marketing myself instead of hiring someone?

Absolutely. Many business owners successfully manage their own Pinterest with the right strategy and time commitment. The question is whether you have 3-5 hours weekly to dedicate to keyword research, pin creation, scheduling, and analytics review. If you do and you'll actually use that time, DIY works. If you won't, hiring someone ensures it actually gets done.

What makes Pinterest different from hiring a general social media manager?

Pinterest operates as a visual search engine, not a social network. The skills that matter - keyword research, SEO optimization, long-form content strategy - differ from Instagram or TikTok management. A social media manager who treats Pinterest like another feed will get mediocre results. You need someone who understands search behavior specifically.
