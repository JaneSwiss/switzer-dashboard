HOW TO MAKE A PINTEREST BOARD THAT ACTUALLY DRIVES TRAFFIC TO YOUR BUSINESS

To make a Pinterest board, click the "+" icon on your profile, select "Board," enter a keyword-rich name, and click "Create." The whole process takes about 30 seconds. But creating a board that actually works for your business - one that shows up in search and sends people to your website - takes a bit more thought.

Most small business owners create boards the way they use Pinterest personally. They name boards things like "Inspiration" or "Ideas" or "Stuff I Like." These names mean nothing to Pinterest's search algorithm and nothing to potential clients trying to find exactly what you offer.

Here's what nobody tells you about Pinterest boards: they're not folders for organizing your pins. They're the foundation of your entire Pinterest strategy. Each board is a category that tells Pinterest what you're about, who you serve, and what searches your content should appear in. Get this wrong and your pins float around without direction. Get it right and you build a system that drives traffic for months without constant posting.

I've watched coaches and service providers spend hours creating beautiful pins that go nowhere - all because their boards were set up like a personal mood board instead of a business tool. This post walks you through how to create boards strategically, optimize them properly, and maintain them so they keep working.

WHY YOUR BOARD NAMES ARE COSTING YOU TRAFFIC

Pinterest is a search engine. People type in what they're looking for, and Pinterest serves up results based on relevance. Your board names are one of the first signals Pinterest uses to understand what your content is about.

When you name a board "Business Stuff" or "Marketing Tips," you're competing with millions of other vague boards using the same generic terms. But when you name a board "Social Media Content Ideas for Life Coaches," you're telling Pinterest exactly who this content is for and what problem it solves.

The difference between a board that gets buried and a board that surfaces in search often comes down to how specific your board name is.

Before you create any board, open Pinterest and type your topic into the search bar. Watch the auto-suggest dropdown - these are actual phrases real people search for. If you're a wedding photographer and you see "wedding photography poses outdoor" in the suggestions, that's a board name waiting to happen. Not "Wedding Inspo." Not "Photo Ideas." The actual phrase people search.

Your board names should read like the keywords your ideal client types in when they're actively looking for solutions. A life coach might create:

  • Confidence Building Tips for Women Entrepreneurs
  • Imposter Syndrome Recovery Strategies
  • Morning Routine Ideas for Busy Business Owners

Each of these matches a search someone is already running. Each positions your content in front of people who are one click away from needing exactly what you offer.

Keep board names under 50 characters so they display fully in search results. Lead with the most important keyword. And never use cute or clever names that sacrifice clarity - Pinterest can't read your mind, and neither can your ideal client.

HOW TO CREATE A BOARD (STEP BY STEP)

There are three ways to create a Pinterest board, and knowing all three saves frustration when you're working across devices or pinning on the go.

Creating from your profile (Desktop):

  • Log into Pinterest on your computer
  • Click your profile picture in the top right corner
  • Click the "+" icon next to "Boards"
  • Select "Board"
  • Enter your keyword-optimized board name
  • Choose visibility: Public (visible to everyone) or Secret (only you can see it)
  • Click "Create"

Creating while saving a pin:

  • Find a pin you want to save
  • Click or tap "Save"
  • Scroll to the bottom of your board list
  • Tap "Create board"
  • Name your new board and save the pin simultaneously

The third method is useful but can lead to poorly named boards if you're in a rush. I recommend creating your core boards strategically from your profile first, then using save-as-you-go for boards you add later.

THE BOARD DESCRIPTION MOST PEOPLE LEAVE BLANK

Pinterest gives you 500 characters to describe what your board is about. Most people ignore this completely. That's wasted real estate.

Your board description is another place Pinterest looks to understand your content. It's also visible to anyone who clicks into your board, which means it's a chance to tell potential clients exactly what they'll find and why it matters to them.

A strong board description includes:

  • A clear statement of what the board contains
  • 2-3 relevant keywords woven in naturally
  • Who the board is for (your ideal client)

Here's an example for a brand photographer's board:

"The best brand photography inspiration for female entrepreneurs. Find poses, outfit ideas, and location concepts for your next business photoshoot. Perfect for coaches, consultants, and online service providers."

That description tells Pinterest this board is about brand photography, targets female entrepreneurs, and relates to coaches and consultants. It uses real keywords without sounding stuffed.

To edit your board description, go to your board, click the pencil icon (or three dots on mobile), select "Edit board," and fill in the description field.

Write descriptions when you create the board - not later. "Later" usually means never, and every day without a description is a day your board is harder to find.

If you're building your Pinterest presence for business, these small optimizations compound over time. A board with a keyword-rich name and description will consistently outperform one with neither.

THE FIVE BOARDS EVERY SERVICE PROVIDER NEEDS FIRST

When you're starting from scratch, decision paralysis is real. Instead of staring at a blank profile wondering what boards to create, start with these five categories that map directly to how people find and hire service providers.

Board 1: Your core service or offer

This board is directly tied to what you sell. A copywriter might name it "Website Copywriting Tips for Small Businesses." A business coach might use "Business Growth Strategies for New Entrepreneurs." Every pin saved here should relate to your primary offer.

Board 2: Behind the scenes or about you

People buy from people. A board that shows your process, workspace, or business journey builds connection. This could be "Meet the Designer" or "Behind the Scenes of a Branding Studio."

Board 3: Aspirational client lifestyle

What does life look like for your ideal client after they work with you? A productivity coach might create "Organized Home Office Ideas." A wellness coach might have "Healthy Morning Routines." This board attracts people who want the transformation you provide.

Board 4: Problem-aware content

Create a board around the problem your clients have before they find you. A virtual assistant might name it "Overwhelmed Business Owner Solutions." A therapist might use "Anxiety Management Techniques." This catches people early in their search journey.

Board 5: Social proof or portfolio

Show your work in action. A wedding photographer creates "Real Weddings by [Your Name]." A web designer uses "Website Design Portfolio." Each pin links back to case studies or portfolio pages on your site.

WHEN AND HOW TO USE BOARD SECTIONS

Board sections are sub-categories within a single board. Pinterest released this feature for users who have boards growing too large to manage - but most beginners don't know it exists.

When should you add sections?

Once a board has more than 50-100 pins, or when you notice the content covers distinctly different themes, sections help both Pinterest and your audience understand what's there.

For example: A "Content Marketing Ideas" board might eventually need sections like:

  • Blog Post Ideas
  • Email Marketing Content
  • Social Media Post Ideas
  • Content Planning Templates

To create a section, click into your board, select "Organize" (desktop) or tap the "+" icon (mobile), and choose "Add Section." Name the section with a descriptive keyword - these section names are searchable too.

Sections also let you reorder your content. You can drag pins into relevant sections and rearrange sections to put your most important content first.

One thing to know: creating sections on a board with hundreds of existing pins is tedious. It's easier to add sections early and sort as you go than to reorganize later. If you're just starting a board you know will grow (like "Instagram Marketing Tips"), consider adding 2-3 sections from the beginning.

SECRET BOARDS FOR BUSINESS (BEYOND HIDING PINS)

Secret boards are invisible to everyone except you (and anyone you explicitly invite). Most people think of them as a place to hide guilty-pleasure pins or plan surprise parties. But for business owners, secret boards have strategic applications.

Content batching:

Create a secret board called "Pins to Schedule" and save all your content there before moving it to a scheduling tool. This keeps your drafts separate from your public profile and gives you a visual overview of what's ready to post.

Competitor research:

Pin examples of competitor content, ads you want to study, or viral pins in your niche to a secret "Research" board. Analyze what's working without cluttering your public boards or tipping off competitors that you're watching.

Client mood boards:

If you work with clients on branding, design, or creative projects, create a secret board for each client. Invite them to collaborate, pin inspiration together, then share the final direction publicly when the project is complete.

If you decide to build out your Pinterest marketing strategy over time, having a secret "Strategy Ideas" board where you save tactics and examples is surprisingly useful for keeping your plans organized.

To make a board secret, toggle the "Keep this board secret" option when creating it. You can also convert an existing public board to secret (or vice versa) by editing the board settings.

BOARD COVERS THAT LOOK PROFESSIONAL

Custom board covers make your Pinterest profile look intentional and branded. When potential clients visit your profile - and they will - cohesive covers signal that you take your business seriously.

Board covers don't affect how Pinterest ranks your boards, but they do affect how people perceive you.

The ideal board cover is 600 x 600 pixels (square). You can design these in Canva using their "Pinterest Board Cover" template.

What makes a good board cover:

  • Uses your brand colors consistently across all covers
  • Includes clear, readable text stating the board topic
  • Keeps the design simple - no busy backgrounds or tiny fonts
  • Matches the visual style of your brand everywhere else

KEEPING YOUR BOARDS ACTIVE (THE MAINTENANCE NOBODY MENTIONS)

Creating boards is step one. Keeping them active is what actually drives traffic over time.

Pinterest's algorithm favors boards that show recent activity. Boards updated within the last three months tend to surface more in search and recommendations. A board you created two years ago and never touched again? It's essentially invisible.

Your ongoing maintenance checklist:

  • Add 2-3 new pins to each active board every month (minimum)
  • Review boards quarterly - archive any that no longer fit your business
  • Update board descriptions if your keywords or positioning have shifted
  • Delete or replace pins that link to broken URLs or outdated content

Set a monthly calendar reminder to audit your boards. Open each one, check that recent pins have been added, and verify your links still work. This takes 30 minutes once you build the habit - and it's the difference between boards that compound and boards that decay.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What's the difference between pins and boards on Pinterest?

A pin is a single piece of content - an image or video that links somewhere. A board is a collection where you save related pins. Think of boards as labeled folders and pins as the documents inside them. Every pin you create or save goes onto a board, and each board has its own name, description, and cover.

How do I add pins to my Pinterest board?

When viewing any pin, click or tap "Save." You'll see a list of your boards - select the one where you want the pin saved. If you're creating your own pin, you choose the destination board during the upload process. You can also move pins between boards later by clicking the pencil icon on the pin and selecting a new board.

Can I turn a board into a section of another board?

Not directly. Pinterest doesn't have a feature to convert an existing board into a section. If you want to consolidate, you'll need to manually move the pins from one board into a section of another board, then delete the now-empty original board.

How do I make a mood board on Pinterest?

Create a new board with a descriptive name (like "Fall Brand Shoot Inspiration" or "Website Design Direction"). Search for images that capture the aesthetic, colors, and feeling you're going for, and save them to your board. For client projects, make the board secret initially and invite the client to collaborate before making it public.

What are Pinterest's biggest downsides for business owners?

Pinterest requires consistency over time - you won't see results from a week of pinning. The platform also prioritizes fresh content, so you can't just set up boards once and forget them. And unlike social media, engagement metrics like comments and shares are lower because Pinterest functions more like search than social. The upside is that pins have a much longer lifespan than Instagram posts or stories.

How many boards should I have on my Pinterest profile?

Quality matters more than quantity. Start with 5-10 highly focused boards that directly relate to your business and ideal client. A scattered profile with 50 boards covering random interests dilutes your authority. Pinterest performs better when your entire profile signals expertise in a specific area rather than being a catch-all for every topic you find interesting.

MAKING YOUR BOARDS WORK FOR YOUR BUSINESS

Knowing how to make a Pinterest board is the easy part. The work that actually moves the needle is treating your boards as strategic assets - not just places to dump pins.

Every board should answer the question: what is my ideal client searching for, and how does this board position my content to be found?

Name your boards using actual keywords your audience types into Pinterest search. Write descriptions that reinforce those keywords and clarify who the board is for. Create covers that make your profile look professional when potential clients click through. And maintain your boards with fresh content so Pinterest keeps surfacing them.

The service providers I see winning on Pinterest aren't the ones with the most boards or the prettiest pins. They're the ones who approached their boards strategically from the start and kept showing up consistently.
