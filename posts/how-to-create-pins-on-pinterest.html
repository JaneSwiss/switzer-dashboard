HOW TO CREATE PINS ON PINTEREST THAT ACTUALLY DRIVE TRAFFIC TO YOUR BUSINESS

To create a pin on Pinterest, click the plus icon in the top right corner of your screen, select "Create Pin," upload your image, add a destination link, write a keyword-rich title and description, choose a relevant board, and hit publish. The whole process takes under five minutes once you know what you're doing.

But here's the thing most tutorials skip over entirely - the pin you just made is only as useful as the strategy behind it. A beautiful graphic with no destination link is a dead end. A clever title with no keywords is invisible to search. And one pin per blog post, repeated forever, tells Pinterest's algorithm you're not creating anything new.

If you're a coach, service provider, or small business owner trying to get real traffic from Pinterest, you need to understand how to create pins that actually work. Not pins that look pretty in your feed. Pins that show up when your ideal clients search, get clicked, and send people to your website where they can book, buy, or sign up.

This post walks you through the complete process - from the design decisions you make before you ever open Pinterest, to the publishing steps, to the batch creation system that makes this sustainable when you're running an actual business.

WHY PINTEREST TREATS PINS DIFFERENTLY THAN ANY OTHER CONTENT YOU CREATE

Pinterest is a search engine disguised as a social media platform. That distinction changes everything about how you approach pin creation.

When you post on Instagram, your content gets pushed to followers and then dies within 48 hours. When you create a pin on Pinterest, that pin enters a searchable database where it can show up in results for months - sometimes years. The lifespan of a single pin can be 100 times longer than an Instagram post.

This means your pin's job is not to get likes or comments. Your pin's job is to get found by people searching for exactly what you offer, then convince them to click through to your website.

Pinterest's algorithm prioritizes what it calls "fresh" content. This doesn't mean you need new ideas every day. It means you need new pin designs - even if they link to the same blog post or product page you've been promoting for months.

So what does this actually look like in practice?

Instead of creating one pin for your lead magnet and sharing it repeatedly, you create five different pin designs. Different images, different text overlays, different color treatments. Each one links to the same opt-in page, but Pinterest sees each as fresh content worth distributing.

Most successful Pinterest accounts publish between 15-25 pins per day by repurposing existing content in new visual formats. That sounds overwhelming until you realize you're not creating 25 new ideas - you're creating 25 different visual takes on content you've already written.

This is where batch creation becomes essential. More on that later.

THE TECHNICAL SPECS THAT ACTUALLY MATTER FOR PIN DESIGN

Before you open Canva or Pinterest's native editor, you need to understand the non-negotiable technical requirements. Get these wrong and your pins get cropped awkwardly, buried in search, or skipped entirely.

Pin dimensions: 1000 x 1500 pixels minimum (2:3 ratio)

This vertical format dominates the Pinterest feed. Pins that don't conform get cut off in ways that make your text unreadable and your images awkward. Every single pin you create should be vertical.

File types: JPG, PNG, or MP4 for video

PNG works best for graphics with text overlays because the text stays crisp. JPG is fine for photo-heavy pins. Video pins should be MP4 format, under 15 seconds for best engagement.

Text size: readable on mobile without zooming

Most people browse Pinterest on their phones. If someone has to squint to read your pin, they scroll past it. Test your designs on your own phone before publishing.

THE STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS FOR CREATING A PIN FROM SCRATCH

Here's the complete workflow from idea to published pin. Follow this exactly and you'll have pins that are technically correct, optimized for search, and ready to drive traffic.

Step 1: Define your pin's destination

Before you design anything, decide where this pin will send people. A specific blog post? Your services page? A lead magnet opt-in? Your Etsy listing?

Every pin needs a destination URL. A pin without a link is a wasted opportunity. Pinterest exists to send people places - make sure you're sending them somewhere that serves your business.

Step 2: Research keywords for this pin

Open Pinterest and type your topic into the search bar. Watch the autocomplete suggestions - these are actual phrases people search for. Write down 3-5 keywords you can incorporate into your pin title and description.

For a post about email marketing, you might note:

  • email marketing for beginners
  • how to start an email list
  • email marketing tips small business
  • grow your email list

Step 3: Design your pin graphic in Canva

Open Canva and search "Pinterest Pin" to find correctly sized templates. Choose one that fits your brand aesthetic or start with a blank 1000 x 1500 canvas.

Add your background (a high-quality stock photo, brand photo, or solid color). Layer your text overlay with the hook or headline. Include your logo or URL. Download as PNG.

Step 4: Upload to Pinterest

Click the plus icon in the top right corner of Pinterest, then select "Create Pin." Drag and drop your image or click to upload. Wait for the upload to complete before moving on.

Step 5: Add your destination link

Paste the full URL of the page you want traffic sent to. Double-check that the link works by opening it in a new tab. If you want to track Pinterest traffic specifically in your analytics, add UTM parameters to the end of your URL.

Step 6: Write your pin title (100 characters maximum)

Front-load your primary keyword in the title. Make it descriptive and searchable, not clever or vague.

Good example: "Morning Routine Checklist for Busy Entrepreneurs | Free PDF"

Bad example: "This Changed Everything For Me 🙌"

Step 7: Write your pin description (500 characters maximum)

The first 50-60 characters appear in search results, so lead with your keywords. Include 2-3 relevant keywords naturally throughout. End with a soft call-to-action like "Click to read" or "Download the free guide."

Do not stuff your description with hashtags. Pinterest deemphasized hashtags years ago - keywords woven into natural sentences work better.

Step 8: Select a board

Choose the most relevant board for this pin. If you don't have one that fits, create a new board with a keyword-rich name. Your board selection affects who sees your pin initially, so this matters more than most people realize.

Step 9: Publish or save as draft

Click "Publish" to post immediately. If you're batch-creating and want to schedule strategically, click "Save as draft" instead. Drafts are saved for 30 days.

HOW TO CREATE MULTIPLE PINS FOR THE SAME CONTENT (THE FRESH PIN STRATEGY)

This is where most business owners leave traffic on the table. They create one pin per blog post and call it done. Pinterest rewards the opposite approach.

For every piece of content you want to drive traffic to, create 3-5 different pin designs. Same destination link, different visual presentations.

Here's what this looks like for a life coach with a free guide called "5 Morning Rituals for Overwhelmed Entrepreneurs":

Pin 1: Bold text overlay on a solid color background - "Morning Rituals That Changed My Business"

Pin 2: Lifestyle image of a woman journaling with subtle text at the bottom

Pin 3: Listicle-style pin showing all 5 rituals as a numbered list

Pin 4: Quote graphic featuring one powerful line from the guide

Pin 5: Before/after style showing chaotic morning vs. peaceful morning

All five pins link to the same opt-in page. But Pinterest sees five pieces of fresh content, each with a chance to show up in different searches and appeal to different scrolling patterns.

This is exactly how top Pinterest accounts publish 15-25 pins per day without creating 15-25 new ideas.

Space out publishing these variations over several days or weeks. Posting all five versions at once looks spammy. Spreading them out keeps fresh content flowing consistently.

If you're running a Pinterest strategy for your small business, this fresh pin approach is the difference between occasional traffic and consistent monthly visitors.

THE BATCH CREATION WORKFLOW THAT MAKES THIS SUSTAINABLE

Creating pins one at a time, whenever you remember, is exhausting and inconsistent. Batch creation solves both problems.

Set aside one session per week (or every two weeks) to create all your pins at once. Here's the workflow:

Block 1: Content inventory (15 minutes)

List the 3-5 pieces of content you want to drive traffic to this month. These might be:

  • Your most important blog post
  • Your lead magnet opt-in page
  • A services page or sales page
  • An Etsy listing or product page
  • A new blog post you just published

Block 2: Keyword research (20 minutes)

For each piece of content, spend 4-5 minutes in Pinterest search noting the autocomplete suggestions and related keywords. Write these down - you'll use them in your titles and descriptions.

Block 3: Design session (60-90 minutes)

Open Canva and batch-design all your pins. For each piece of content, create 3-5 variations. Use templates to speed this up - change the image, tweak the text angle, adjust the color, but keep the same basic structure.

When you're done, you should have 15-25 pin graphics ready to go.

Block 4: Upload and schedule (30 minutes)

Upload all your pins to Pinterest, either publishing immediately or saving as drafts. If you use a scheduling tool like Tailwind, load them into your queue and let the tool spread them out over the coming weeks.

This entire process takes 2-3 hours and gives you weeks of fresh content. Compare that to trying to create a pin every day - the batch approach wins every time for busy business owners.

WHAT MAKES A PIN GET CLICKED (NOT JUST SAVED)

Saves are nice. Clicks pay the bills.

A high-performing pin does two things: it stops the scroll with visual appeal, then it convinces someone to click through for more. Here's what drives that click:

Clear benefit in the text overlay

Your pin text should answer one question: "What will I get if I click this?" Not "What is this about?" but "What's in it for me?"

Weak: "Email Marketing Tips"

Strong: "The Email Sequence That Converts Cold Subscribers Into Buyers"

Curiosity without clickbait

You want someone to think "I need to know more" - not "This is probably overhyped garbage." The difference is specificity. Vague claims feel like clickbait. Specific promises feel like value.

A destination that delivers

Nothing kills your Pinterest performance faster than pins that send people to irrelevant or disappointing pages. If your pin promises "5 Morning Rituals," the landing page better deliver exactly that - not a generic homepage where visitors have to hunt for the content.

THE COMMON MISTAKES THAT MAKE PINS INVISIBLE

After reviewing hundreds of Pinterest accounts from coaches and service providers, I see the same mistakes repeatedly. Avoid these and you're already ahead of most of your competitors:

Mistake 1: No destination link

It sounds basic, but I see pins with no link attached constantly. Pinterest's entire value proposition is sending people to websites. A pin without a link serves no business purpose.

Mistake 2: Linking everything to your homepage

Your homepage is designed to give visitors options. A pin should send someone to a specific page that matches exactly what the pin promised. If your pin is about email marketing tips, link to your email marketing blog post - not your generic homepage.

Mistake 3: Horizontal or square images

Pinterest is a vertical feed. Horizontal images take up less space, get cropped awkwardly, and consistently underperform. Always use 2:3 ratio vertical pins.

Mistake 4: Treating Pinterest like Instagram

Hashtag-heavy captions, casual conversational tone without keywords, no destination links - all common on Instagram, all wrong for Pinterest. Pinterest is search-first. Optimize accordingly.

Mistake 5: Creating one pin per piece of content

One and done means you're relying on that single design to capture all possible search traffic. Multiple variations give you multiple chances to get found. Always create 3-5 pins per piece of content.

Mistake 6: Text too small to read on mobile

If you're designing on a large monitor, your text probably looks fine. Shrink it down to phone size and see if it's still readable. Most Pinterest browsing happens on mobile devices.

HOW VIDEO PINS AND IDEA PINS FIT INTO YOUR STRATEGY

Pinterest offers more than static image pins. Understanding when to use each format helps you create the right content for your goal.

Static image pins are the workhorse of Pinterest marketing. They drive website traffic, they show up in search results, and they have the longest lifespan. If your goal is getting people to your website, static pins should make up the majority of your content.

Video pins get higher engagement and watch time than static pins. They're excellent for tutorials, product demonstrations, or behind-the-scenes content. Keep videos under 15 seconds and make sure the first frame is compelling - most people scroll past without turning on sound.

Idea pins (Pinterest's multi-page format) are designed to build followers and keep people on the platform. Historically, Idea Pins haven't included clickable destination links, which limits their direct traffic value. They're best used for brand awareness and growing your Pinterest following rather than driving website visits.

For coaches and service providers focused on getting clients, static image pins with clear destination links should be your priority. Add video pins when you have tutorial content that works in video format. Use Idea Pins sparingly for community building, but don't expect them to drive traffic directly.

Understanding the best times to post different content types can also improve how each format performs in your overall strategy.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How do I create a pin for the first time?

Click the plus icon in the top right corner of Pinterest, select "Create Pin," upload your image (1000 x 1500 pixels works best), paste your destination link, write a keyword-rich title and description, select a relevant board, and click publish. The whole process takes under five minutes once you have your image ready.

What's the difference between a regular pin and an Idea Pin?

A regular pin is a single image or video that links to an external website - this is what drives traffic to your business. An Idea Pin is a multi-page format (like Instagram Stories) designed to keep users on Pinterest; it builds engagement and followers but historically hasn't included clickable external links.

Can creating pins on Pinterest actually make money?

Pinterest itself doesn't pay you for views or engagement. The money comes from what happens after someone clicks your pin - when they land on your website, opt into your email list, book your services, or buy your products. Pins are a traffic source, not a direct income stream.

How can I create pins in bulk without spending hours every day?

Use the batch creation method: set aside one 2-3 hour session weekly or biweekly to create all your pins at once. Design 3-5 variations for each piece of content in Canva, then upload them all to Pinterest as drafts or schedule them using a tool like Tailwind.

How do I make sure my pin shows an image and not a video?

When creating a pin, simply upload a static image file (JPG or PNG) instead of a video file (MP4). If you're using the Pinterest mobile app camera feature, make sure you're selecting "Photo" rather than "Video" before capturing content.

What makes a pin show up in Pinterest search results?

Pinterest uses keywords in your pin title, description, and board name to determine what searches your pin appears in. Research actual search terms using Pinterest's autocomplete suggestions, then incorporate those keywords naturally into your pin copy. The first 50-60 characters of your description are most important for search visibility.

YOUR PINS ARE ONLY AS GOOD AS YOUR SYSTEM

Knowing how to create pins on Pinterest is the easy part. The mechanics take five minutes to learn. What separates accounts that drive consistent traffic from accounts that get nothing is the system behind the pins.

Fresh content beats repeated content. Multiple pin variations beat single designs. Keywords beat clever copy. And consistency - even at a modest pace - beats sporadic bursts of activity followed by months of silence.

You don't need to pin 25 times a day to see results. But you do need to create pins intentionally, with clear destination links, keyword-optimized copy, and a batch creation workflow that makes showing up sustainable.

If you've been treating Pinterest like an afterthought - pinning randomly when you remember, using the same graphic for months, or skipping destination links entirely - start fresh. Pick one piece of content you want to drive traffic to, create five pin variations this week, and publish them over the next few weeks.

That simple shift will teach you more about what works than any tutorial.
