HOW TO DELETE A BOARD ON PINTEREST

To delete a board on Pinterest, open the board, tap the three-dot menu, select "Edit board," scroll to the bottom, and tap "Delete board." The whole process takes about thirty seconds - but that simple action triggers a chain of consequences that most business owners don't think about until it's too late.

Here's what happens the moment you confirm: every single pin on that board gets deleted too. Every follower you've built on that board disappears. And you have exactly seven days to change your mind before everything vanishes permanently.

For service providers, coaches, and small business owners who've spent months (or years) curating boards that drive traffic to their websites, this isn't just a cleanup task. It's a strategic decision that can impact your Pinterest visibility and your bottom line.

The real question isn't how to delete a board on Pinterest - it's whether you should delete it at all.

Most of the time, there's a better option. And the difference between deleting, archiving, and making a board secret can mean the difference between losing years of work and simply hiding it from view while you figure out your next move.

Let me walk you through exactly what to do - and more importantly, when to do it.

The decision framework before you touch that delete button

Before you delete anything, run through this quick audit. I've watched too many business owners delete boards impulsively during a "profile cleanup" mood - then regret it a week later when they realize what they lost.

Does this board still align with your current offers?

If you're a business coach who used to post fitness content, those old workout boards might feel embarrassing. But embarrassment isn't a good enough reason to delete. If there's even a chance you might pivot back or expand your services, archive instead.

Has this board driven any website traffic in the past 90 days?

Check your Pinterest analytics. If a board is still sending clicks to your site - even small numbers - it's working for you while you sleep. Deleting an active board is like unplugging a machine that's still running.

How many followers does this board have?

Board followers are gone forever when you delete. There's no way to recover them, transfer them to another board, or even know who they were. If you've built up 200+ followers on a niche board, that's an audience you've earned. Think carefully before walking away from it.

Could you repurpose this board with a new title and description?

Sometimes the board itself isn't the problem - it's just outdated. You can change a board's name, description, and cover image without losing your pins or followers. A board called "Marketing Tips 2024" can become "Marketing Tips for Service Providers" with a few edits.

If you've answered these questions and you're still certain you want to delete, keep reading.

How to delete a Pinterest board on desktop

Here's the step-by-step process for deleting a board using Pinterest in your browser. This works whether you're on a Mac or PC.

Step 1: Log into your Pinterest business account at pinterest.com

Step 2: Click your profile picture in the top-right corner

Step 3: Select "Saved" to view all your boards

Step 4: Click on the specific board you want to delete

Step 5: Click the three-dot icon (ellipsis) below the board title

Step 6: Select "Edit board" from the dropdown menu

Step 7: Scroll to the bottom of the edit panel

Step 8: Click "Delete board"

Step 9: Confirm by clicking "Delete" in the popup warning

After seven days? Gone. No appeals. No customer service ticket. No recovery.

How to delete a Pinterest board on mobile

The mobile process is slightly different depending on whether you're using iOS or Android, but the core steps are the same.

Step 1: Open the Pinterest app

Step 2: Tap your profile icon (bottom-right corner)

Step 3: Tap the board you want to delete

Step 4: Tap the three-dot icon (top-right corner of screen)

Step 5: Tap "Edit board"

Step 6: Scroll down and tap "Delete board"

Step 7: Confirm by tapping "Delete"

One thing to watch for on mobile: the "Edit board" option can be easy to miss if you're scrolling quickly. The three-dot menu has several options, and "Edit board" isn't always at the top of the list.

Also, the "Recently deleted" section is harder to find on mobile. To restore a deleted board from your phone, go to your profile, scroll all the way to the bottom past your boards, and look for a section labeled "Recently deleted." Tap the board you want to restore, then tap "Restore."

If you're doing a major cleanup - deleting multiple boards - I'd recommend doing it on desktop where the interface is clearer and it's harder to accidentally tap the wrong thing.

Why archiving is almost always better for business owners

Let me be direct: if you're running a business account, archiving should be your default choice, not deleting.

Here's the difference:

When you archive a board, it disappears from your public profile. Visitors can't see it. You can't save new pins to it. But everything is preserved - your pins, your followers, your board description, all of it. You can unarchive at any time and everything comes back exactly as it was.

When you delete a board, you lose everything. Permanently.

For business owners, this matters more than you might think. Pinterest boards with keyword-rich titles and descriptions contribute to how easily people find you on the platform. Your boards are essentially category pages that help Pinterest understand what your account is about and who to show your content to.

Deleting established boards can temporarily affect your reach - not dramatically, but enough that if you're relying on Pinterest for website traffic, you should think twice before mass-deleting boards during a rebrand.

The Pinterest Business Community actually recommends archiving over deleting: "If a Board no longer fits your brand, just archive it instead of deleting. Archiving hides it from public view but keeps all your content."

To archive instead of delete:

  • Follow steps 1-6 from the desktop deletion process above
  • Instead of clicking "Delete board," toggle ON "Archive this board"
  • Click "Done"
  • Find archived boards at the bottom of your profile under "Archived boards"

How to delete a board section without losing your pins

This is one of the most common questions I see, and most tutorials skip it entirely.

Board sections are the folders within a board - they let you organize pins into subcategories. For example, a "Business Marketing" board might have sections for "Social Media Tips," "Email Marketing," and "Content Ideas."

You can delete a section without deleting the entire board. And here's the key part: you can keep your pins when you delete a section.

Here's how:

Step 1: Open the board containing the section

Step 2: Click or tap on the section name

Step 3: Click or tap the three-dot icon

Step 4: Select "Edit section"

Step 5: Choose "Delete section"

Step 6: Pinterest will ask: Move pins to main board OR delete pins

Step 7: Select "Move to [board name]" to keep your pins

Step 8: Confirm your choice

How to leave a group board you don't own

Here's a scenario that confuses a lot of people: you joined a group board months or years ago, and now it's cluttering your profile or no longer fits your brand. But when you try to delete it, you can't find the option.

That's because you can only delete boards you created. If you're a collaborator on someone else's group board, your only option is to leave.

Here's how:

Step 1: Open the group board

Step 2: Click or tap the three-dot icon

Step 3: Select "Leave board"

Step 4: Confirm your choice

When you leave a group board, any pins you saved to it while you were a member stay on the board - they don't come with you. However, those pins are also still saved in your "All Pins" section, so you haven't lost them entirely.

If you're the owner of a group board and want to remove it completely, you can delete it the same way you'd delete any other board. When you delete a group board as the owner, all collaborators lose access and all pins are deleted.

One thing to consider: if you built a group board with valuable collaborators and it's gotten off-track, you might be better off removing specific collaborators or adjusting the board settings rather than deleting everything. You can change who can save to the board from the "Edit board" menu.

The profile refresh checklist for service providers

Deleting boards should be part of a larger strategy, not a random cleanup session. Here's the approach I recommend for coaches, consultants, and service-based business owners who want to refresh their Pinterest presence without destroying what's working.

Step 1: Audit your current boards

Go through every board and categorize it:

  • Keep: Aligns with current offers, drives traffic, has engaged followers
  • Archive: Doesn't fit current brand but might be useful later
  • Delete: Test boards, duplicates, or boards with zero engagement and no strategic value
  • Repurpose: Good content, but needs a new title or description to match your current positioning

Step 2: Check your analytics first

Before you archive or delete anything, look at your Pinterest analytics for the past 90 days. Sort by outbound clicks. Any board that's sending people to your website is working for you - even if it feels outdated.

Step 3: Start with archives, not deletions

Archive everything in your "Archive" and "Delete" categories first. Give it 30 days. If you don't miss any of those boards and your traffic stays stable, then consider permanent deletion.

Step 4: Repurpose before you remove

For boards in your "Repurpose" category, update the title and description with current keywords relevant to your business. Change the cover image. You've kept all your pins and followers while essentially creating a new board.

The 80/20 rule applies to Pinterest boards: about 20% of your boards probably drive 80% of your traffic. Your job is to figure out which ones they are and protect them - not delete everything that doesn't look perfect.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I delete a Pinterest pin?

You can only delete pins you created or saved yourself. If you're trying to delete someone else's pin from a group board, you don't have permission - only the person who saved it or the board owner can remove it. If you're trying to delete your own pin and the option isn't appearing, try refreshing the page or updating your Pinterest app.

How do I delete a board without deleting the pins?

You can't delete a board while keeping the pins on that specific board - deletion removes everything. However, you can move all the pins to a different board before deleting. Open the board, select all pins (or select them in batches), choose "Move," pick a new destination board, then delete the now-empty original board.

Can you permanently delete Pinterest boards?

Yes, but not immediately. When you delete a board, it moves to "Recently deleted" for seven days. After seven days with no action, it's permanently deleted and cannot be recovered. If you want to skip the waiting period, you can go to "Recently deleted" and manually delete the board permanently from there.

Does deleting a board on Pinterest delete the pins?

Yes. When you delete a board, every pin saved to that board is deleted along with it. This includes pins in board sections. The only exception is if those pins are also saved to other boards - in that case, the pins remain on the other boards but are removed from the deleted one.

How do I remove a board I didn't create from my profile?

If you're a collaborator on someone else's group board, you can leave it - but you can't delete it. Go to the board, tap the three-dot menu, and select "Leave board." This removes it from your profile but doesn't affect the board itself or other collaborators.

How do I mass delete multiple boards at once?

Pinterest doesn't offer a native bulk deletion feature. You have to delete boards one at a time through the standard process. For major account overhauls, some users turn to third-party tools like Redact, which offers mass deletion of pins and boards - but use external tools carefully and understand what permissions you're granting.

Making the right choice for your business

Knowing how to delete a board on Pinterest takes thirty seconds to learn. Knowing when to delete - and when to use a smarter alternative - takes a bit more thought.

For most business owners, archiving is the safer default. You get the clean profile you want without losing content you might need later. Deletion should be reserved for boards that genuinely have no value: test boards, duplicates, or content that's completely irrelevant to any version of your business.

Before you start your cleanup, check your analytics. Identify which boards actually drive traffic. Protect those at all costs - even if they don't fit your "aesthetic" perfectly. A board that sends 50 people a month to your website is more valuable than a beautifully curated board nobody clicks.
