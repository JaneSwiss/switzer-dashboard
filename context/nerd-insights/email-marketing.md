## Building Your List From 5,000 to 100,000+ Subscribers

Dylan Jarris, who spent 13 years in e-commerce including managing hundred-million-dollar portfolios at Zappos and Zulily, grew his email list from 5,000 to over 100,000 subscribers. The mechanics behind this growth involve three core platform choices and one critical design consideration.

**Platform options for collecting and sending:**
- **Klaviyo** — best for e-commerce with deep integrations
- **Mailchimp** — broad feature set, widely supported
- **Flodesk** — visually focused, popular with creative businesses

The design constraint that matters most: over 70% of people shop on mobile. This means links outperform QR codes in emails. QR codes require a second device to scan — if someone's reading your email on their phone, they can't scan a QR code on that same phone. Always default to tappable links.

**For Switzertemplates:** Your branding kits include social media templates that can double as email header graphics. When someone buys a branding kit, their email signup is a natural next step — offer a bonus template set or a "quick-start guide" as the incentive to join.

<a name="content-philosophy"></a>
## The "Give Until They Ask" Content Philosophy

Two frameworks shape how successful email marketers think about content ratio:

> Gary Vee's "jab jab jab right hook" and Hormozi's "give give give until they ask" approach

Both point to the same mechanism: you earn the right to sell by providing value first. The pattern is roughly 3-4 value emails for every promotional email.

**What "value" looks like in practice:**

| Type of Value Email | Example for Product Sellers |
|---------------------|----------------------------|
| Educational | "How to photograph your products with just your phone" |
| Behind-the-scenes | "Here's how I package orders — and why it matters" |
| Curated content | "5 accounts I'm following for design inspiration right now" |
| Free resource | A checklist, template, or mini-guide |

The "right hook" or "ask" is your promotional email — a product launch, a sale, a bundle offer. But it lands better when it follows genuine helpfulness.

**For Switzertemplates:** Your audience of female small business owners and coaches respond to content that makes their business look more professional with less effort. Value emails could include: "3 Instagram post ideas you can create in 10 minutes with Canva" or "The exact fonts I'd pair together for a coaching brand." Each email positions your templates as the shortcut.

<a name="mobile-first-mechanics"></a>
## Mobile-First Email Mechanics

The 70%+ mobile shopping stat has tactical implications beyond just "use links instead of QR codes."

**Structural changes for mobile readers:**

1. **Keep subject lines under 40 characters** — longer gets cut off on phone screens
2. **Front-load your main point** — assume they'll only read the first two lines
3. **Single-column layouts only** — two-column designs break on mobile
4. **Buttons over text links** — larger tap targets reduce friction
5. **Images should be optional** — many email clients block images by default; your message needs to work without them

The mechanism: mobile readers are scanning, not studying. They're checking email while waiting for coffee, between meetings, during commutes. Your email competes with every other notification on their screen. Structure it so the value is obvious in under 3 seconds.

## How Email Fits Into a Multi-Channel Funnel

Email marketing doesn't exist in isolation — it works best as the "owned" layer that connects other traffic sources. Dylan's approach shows how different channels feed into email:

**The funnel logic:**

1. **Discovery happens elsewhere** — Etsy, Pinterest, Instagram, Facebook groups, TikTok
2. **First transaction might happen on platform** — Etsy, TikTok Shop, Instagram Shop
3. **Email captures the relationship** — post-purchase follow-up moves them to your list
4. **Future transactions happen direct** — your website, with no platform fees

> Dylan made over $100,000 from website within first 12 years without ads by funneling Etsy traffic to Shopify

The bridge is critical: you can't tell Etsy customers to buy on your website directly (that's fee avoidance and violates terms). But you can use social media as the intermediate step — your Instagram bio leads to your email signup, your email leads to your website.

**For Switzertemplates:** If someone discovers you through a marketplace like Etsy or Creative Market, your email list is how you convert a one-time buyer into a repeat customer. A post-purchase email sequence introducing your other products (branding kit → website template → 3-in-1 bundle) increases lifetime value without additional ad spend.

## Email vs. DM Automation: When Each Works Better

Dylan makes a striking claim about where he sees higher engagement now:

> Dylan considers ManyChat more effective than email marketing currently

ManyChat is Instagram DM automation — you set a trigger word in your caption, someone comments that word, and ManyChat automatically DMs them with links, photos, messages, or voice memos. You can build nurture sequences with delays (2 hours, 1 day, 4 days, 1 month later).

**When DM automation beats email:**
- Higher open rates (DMs are seen; emails often aren't)
- More personal feel — it's a conversation, not a broadcast
- Works especially well for impulse-driven content and low-price products

**When email still wins:**
- You own the list — if Instagram changes its algorithm or bans ManyChat, you lose nothing with email
- Better for longer content, detailed guides, proper design
- More professional for service-based businesses and B2B

The mechanism isn't either/or — it's which stage of the relationship. DMs work for initial engagement and quick conversions. Email works for long-term nurturing and larger purchases.

**For Switzertemplates:** Your $15 Instagram templates could sell well through ManyChat — someone comments "TEMPLATES" and gets an instant DM with a preview and link. Your $82 bundle needs more context; that's where a proper email sequence with visuals and social proof makes sense.

## Realistic Timeline and Choosing Your Focus

Dylan's core advice across all 12 traffic methods he teaches, including email:

> Implement 2-3 methods rather than all 12

For email specifically, expect this timeline:
- **Week 1-2:** Platform setup, first welcome sequence written
- **Month 1:** Your first value emails go out; you're learning what resonates
- **Month 2-3:** You start seeing which subject lines get opens, which content gets clicks
- **Month 6+:** Compounding effect kicks in as your list grows and you refine your approach

Email is a slow-burn channel. Unlike Facebook groups (where Dylan's video editor got 100+ orders in 24 hours from a single post), email builds value over time. The payoff is ownership — you're not renting attention from a platform.

**For Switzertemplates:** Pair email with one fast channel and one compounding channel. Fast: Facebook groups where your audience already gathers (interior design groups, small business owner communities). Compounding: Pinterest using the 7x7 strategy (7 pins daily, 7 days weekly), which brought Dylan over 500,000 free visits. Email ties them together and captures the relationship long-term.
