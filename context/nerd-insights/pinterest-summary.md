# Pinterest — What the Nerd Agent Has Learned

*Consolidated from 10 sources (1 newsletter + 9 YouTube videos) — every point below links back to the exact source it came from, so you can verify or go deeper on anything. A faithful account of what these sources say, merged into one summary with overlap removed — no added interpretation. See the [action checklist](pinterest-action-checklist.md) for the agent's own suggestions on applying it.*

## The platform, in numbers

- Pinterest has **600+ million monthly active users** and 275 million weekly active users; over 70% are women, though male user growth is accelerating in fashion, fitness, and home improvement. 73% of Pinterest users say its visual search beats traditional text search. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- **96% of Pinterest searches are unbranded** — almost everyone searching is open to discovering a seller they've never heard of, not hunting for a specific brand name. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- Pins now also **rank inside Google search results**, so Pinterest SEO work pays twice. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))

## Why pins outlast everything else

- Average pin lifespan is **4+ months**, with a half-life of **13+ months** — versus 21-48 hours for an Instagram post and just 5-6 hours on Facebook. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA), [Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- One creator says pins she published **8-11 years ago** are still driving traffic she values at $30,000-$60,000 each. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- Another account stopped posting completely and **still generated inquiries three years later**. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- A six-year Pinterest blogger says that even after deliberately cutting back her pinning, her monthly page views from Pinterest "have barely changed" — still hundreds of thousands a month. ([6-Year Blogger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QkXSH_cLPQ4))

## You don't need an audience — you need to be findable

- Nick Garcia took a brand-new account, four months old, with only **45 followers**, to **1 million impressions** and **350-600 website visits a day**. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- One student of the Etsy-sellers boot camp (SK Carr) hit **over 1 million Pinterest views within 60 days** of starting from scratch. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))

## Keyword research — the exact methods and tools named

- **Autocomplete method**: type a broad keyword into Pinterest's search bar and record what the dropdown suggests. **Alphabet-soup method**: add each letter A-Z after a keyword to surface 20-30 longtail variations per topic. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- **Pinterest Trends**: free, built into Pinterest, shows search-volume graphs over a chosen date range plus related-keyword suggestions — sources recommend setting the range to a full year and creating content **45-60 days before a seasonal peak**. The newsletter walks through scanning trend data month-by-month (e.g. setting the end date to 31 July, then 30 Sept, 31 Oct, 30 Nov, 31 Dec of the prior year) to map out a whole season's worth of keyword timing. (Tony Hills Newsletters, [Strategy That's Working #2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m9vnKPnVocA), [Strategy That's Working #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfOhKeciZpU))
- **Interest Explorer**: surfaces official Pinterest "interest" categories (used for board names) — when a search returns nothing, broaden the term (e.g. "day hike first aid kit" → "first aid kit" revealed "hiking first aid kit" and "hiking first aid kit checklist" as real interest categories). (Tony Hills Newsletters)
- **PinClicks** is named repeatedly as the strongest dedicated keyword-research tool — e.g. its "Keyword Explorer" gave actual scored data for "day hike" (rainy day hike outfit: 200, multi-day hike packing list: 151, day hike food: 149…), and its "Keyword Planner" cluster report for "cottagecore" returned 10 clusters covering 279 keywords (top cluster: "Cottagecore Living Room," score 25, volume 62,384). It can also reveal which annotations the *current* top-ranking pins for a term carry. (Tony Hills Newsletters, [Fix This First](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zE0d-LA2yOI))
- **Tailwind's free keyword tool**: enter a keyword or a URL and it returns related top keywords plus a "resonance" score (how relevant + actively promoted by Pinterest a term is) — useful since Pinterest only publishes search-volume numbers for select keywords. ([Grow on Pinterest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0auLgo1azlE))
- One source's practical AI shortcut: prompt ChatGPT or Claude with *"Give me SEO and clickable Pinterest titles for the topic [your topic]"* — then shorten/tweak the output, since AI-generated titles tend to run too long. ([Growing My Blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_MbkHhAeF4))
- The core discipline behind all of it: keywords have to mirror how the **audience** actually searches (e.g. "living room wall art"), not how the business owner privately describes their own work. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))

## Annotations — Pinterest's hidden tagging layer

- Every pin gets invisible keyword tags ("annotations") assigned by Pinterest from signals including: pin title, description, URL, board name, board description, page title/description, **and objects its AI detects inside the image itself**. Annotations are visible when logged *out* of Pinterest (or via PinClicks, which shows the top 25 ranking pins' annotations and how often each occurs). (Tony Hills Newsletters)
- Pinterest now actively scores pin **diversity** across three axes: topic (interest keywords), intent (shopping / planning / saving), and style (visual look, design, aesthetic) — which is why "same-ish" pins (same design, new title) are no longer treated as distinct content. (Tony Hills Newsletters)

## Exact pin specs named across sources

- Aspect ratio **2:3** = **1000 × 1500 px** — vertical pins dominate the feed. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- Frontload the keyword in the **first 30-40 characters** of the title (Pinterest truncates on mobile); the **first 50-60 characters** of the description are what shows before "read more." ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- **99% of top-performing pins (by outbound clicks) carry text overlay** — designs without text underperform. Faces on pins generally underperform too, *except* in fashion/makeup/jewellery niches. ([Growing My Blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_MbkHhAeF4))
- Lifestyle/in-context mockups beat sterile white-background product shots; original photos and simple real-life demos now outperform generic stock or heavily-edited graphics, because Pinterest's AI reads the image content directly. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes), [Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c))
- Infographic-style pins **generate saves but not clicks** — to actually drive traffic, a pin has to create curiosity and promise an outcome rather than list features (example given: *"Plan a month of content in 30 minutes"* beat *"Social Media Plan PDF"*). ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- Video pins need a hook in the **first 3 seconds**, should assume sound-off viewing, and lean on text overlay to carry the message; short before/after or walk-through clips are named as the strongest current format. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA), [Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c))

## "Fresh pins" — the technique repeated most often

- Pinterest specifically rewards **"fresh pins"**: new image + new title + new description pointing at the *same* existing product or URL. Sources are explicit this needs **no new product and no new content** — just a new presentation of what already exists. Recommended volume: **5-10, sometimes up to 20, distinct pins per single product or URL.** ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes), [Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c))
- Two concrete pinning "recipes" were given: (1) **3 pins per product**, each with a different photo/SEO/hashtags, posted **one per week across three weeks** — 5 products = 15 photos = 15 pins ([Strategy That's Working #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfOhKeciZpU)); (2) **6 pins per blog post** — two pins for each of three keyword-set "boards" (one existing board + two new ones named exactly after the keyword sets), staggered one per week, rotating boards A→B→C→A — meaning one piece of content stays "covered" for 5-6 weeks before it needs attention again. ([Strategy That's Working #2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m9vnKPnVocA))
- Recommended frequency: **1-2 fresh pins a day** as a baseline (some sources say up to 5-10/day; one names **10-12/day as the bare minimum** for a blog-growth strategy) — but every source agrees that **rotating/spacing matters more than raw volume**: e.g. pin URL A on Monday, URL B Tuesday, back to A Wednesday — never the same link twice in a row. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes), [Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c), [Growing My Blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_MbkHhAeF4))
- The blunt version of "consistency over volume": **it's better to post 1 pin daily than 30 in one monthly burst** — and if 5 pins a week is your honest ceiling, do exactly 5 every week rather than swinging between 1, 5, and 20. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA), [6-Year Blogger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QkXSH_cLPQ4))

## Where to send the click — and why it matters

- Linking a pin to your **whole shop/homepage rather than a specific product page** was named directly as something that produces *favourites but not conversions*. ([Strategy That's Working #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfOhKeciZpU))
- For **Etsy** specifically: link straight to the listing — items that reach an Etsy cart keep reappearing to the customer when they return to the site, pulling them back toward the purchase. For **Shopify**: link straight to the **checkout page** so the customer can complete the purchase immediately via Apple Pay/PayPal. ([Strategy That's Working #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfOhKeciZpU))
- Pins can point to *any* URL: Etsy listings, Shopify pages, personal sites, blog posts, podcasts, even Instagram profiles. ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))

## Boards and the 80/20 content mix

- Name boards after the **exact phrases people search** / official Pinterest "interest" categories — e.g. "Day Hikes" and "First Aid Kits," not "Outdoor Stuff." (Tony Hills Newsletters, [Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- Seed each **new** board with **15-20 repinned, relevant, high-quality pins** before adding original content, to establish it. ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- Recommended content mix: **80% evergreen / 20% seasonal-trending.** ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA))
- One outdated myth corrected directly: you do **not** need to save 80% other people's pins / 20% your own — Pinterest has confirmed accounts can save **100% their own content**. ([Grow on Pinterest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0auLgo1azlE))

## Diagnosing *why* traffic stalls — a named 6-problem framework

One source ("Fix This First") laid out a specific diagnostic model — that traffic problems are a **signal problem, not a design problem** — with six causes and matching fixes: ([Fix This First](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zE0d-LA2yOI))

1. **Not enough data** (account too new / too low-volume → zero distribution) → "pin more, but smartly"
2. **Low signal density** (inconsistent posting confuses the algorithm's categorisation) → pin on a regular schedule
3. **Weak pin curiosity** (no gap between what the viewer expects and already knows → no click) → sharper, smarter keyword-led titles/descriptions
4. **Traffic leak** (high bounce rate — leaving within ~10 seconds — tells Pinterest the content wasn't valuable, triggering a reach penalty) → stronger opening hooks on landing pages + "related post" prompts to keep visitors moving
5. **Guessing keywords** (intuition instead of data → answering questions nobody asks) → use **pinclicks.com**, named directly as "the best tool on the market for Pinterest search data"
6. **No feedback loop** (posting blind, repeating the same mistakes) → check analytics regularly and double down on what's already working

(That same account reports **180,000 outbound clicks over 90 days** from a single beauty-niche account once these signals were fixed — described as "consistent growth month over month.")

## Handling algorithm changes (from a 6-year Pinterest veteran)

- When stats drop after an algorithm change: **wait about a week first** — "90% of the time," numbers recover on their own.
- If they're still down after a week, change **only one thing** and give it about **3 weeks** to show results (Pinterest itself can take up to 3 months to fully roll a change out, but 3 weeks is workable for a read).
- **Never overhaul the whole strategy at once** during a change — you lose the ability to tell what was actually working. ([6-Year Blogger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QkXSH_cLPQ4))
- The same source stresses **niche clarity speeds growth** — an account Pinterest can clearly identify as "about dogs" grows faster than one it reads as "about animals generally" — and recommends mixing native Pinterest-made pins (in Canva) with repurposed video content (e.g. from TikTok), since "Pinterest goes through waves" of favouring one format over the other.

## Tools named, specifically

- **Pin Generator** — paste a link or upload an image, generate **10-20 pin variations in minutes**, ready to schedule every other day or so. ([Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c), [Growing My Blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_MbkHhAeF4))
- **Pinterest's native scheduler** — schedules up to two weeks at a time, found under the "Created"/"Saved" tab; multiple sources directly compared it against Tailwind and live posting and found **no meaningful difference in distribution** between any of the three. ([Strategy That's Working #2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m9vnKPnVocA), [Grow on Pinterest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0auLgo1azlE), [Growing My Blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I_MbkHhAeF4))
- **Tailwind Turbo Pin** (beta) — requires 10 engagements (likes/comments/saves) with other users' pins before you can submit your own to its feed; one creator's single Turbo Pin (submitted Dec 5) became their best-performing pin of the month: 521 impressions, 11 engagements, 1 save. **Tailwind Communities** are a separate feature focused on niche-relevant engagement, unlike Turbo Pin. ([Grow on Pinterest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0auLgo1azlE))
- **Pinterest Predicts** — an annual trend report Pinterest itself publishes every December; sources say its predictions "mostly come true." ([Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c))
- Reporting metrics named as the ones that matter: **reach, saves, outbound clicks, and year-over-year (not just month-over-month) growth.** ([Grow on Pinterest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0auLgo1azlE))

## Setting realistic expectations

- The whole system is repeatedly costed at **roughly one hour a week**, against the ~19-20 hours/week the average entrepreneur spends on marketing overall — and if it proves out, the entire routine can be handed to a VA at an estimated **$30-35/hour** (so, in the ballpark of $140/month). ([Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- Expect **30-60 days** before traffic visibly picks up, and **1-2 months** before analytics become genuinely readable. Pinterest also has a "warm-up" period — early pins can sit at low impressions and then suddenly take off weeks later. ([Strategy That's Working #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfOhKeciZpU), [Getting Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnScrkLAX9c))
- **Most people quit in months 1-3** — but multiple sources independently place the point where the "flywheel" actually kicks in at **months 4-6.** ([Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYKnvkrIevA), [Etsy Sellers Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7ZqpgTpjes))
- New pins/accounts can now rank for a term in as little as **2-4 weeks** — much faster than the old "months" guidance — though *niche changes the timing*: knitting audiences search for handmade Christmas gifts months ahead, while recipe searches cluster close to the holiday, often starting after Thanksgiving. (Tony Hills Newsletters)
