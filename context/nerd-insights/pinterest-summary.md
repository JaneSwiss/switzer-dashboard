# Pinterest — What the Nerd Agent Has Learned

*Consolidated from 7 sources (1 newsletter + 6 YouTube videos) — all about Pinterest growth and strategy. A faithful account of what these sources say, merged into one summary with overlap removed. No added interpretation — see the [action checklist](pinterest-action-checklist.md) for the agent's own suggestions.*

---

**Pinterest is a search engine, not a social network.**
Pins have an average lifespan of 4+ months and a half-life of 13+ months — compared to 24-48 hours for an Instagram post. People arrive on Pinterest already searching and planning, with high purchase intent, not passively scrolling. One source described it as "a quiet system running in the background": an account that stopped posting still generated inquiries three years later.

**You don't need followers or fame to get traffic.**
Discovery is search-based, not audience-based. Sources cite an account with just 45 followers generating 350-600 daily website visits, and new accounts reaching a million impressions within four months. Etsy sellers specifically can see Pinterest show up as a free organic traffic source in their shop analytics, alongside paid channels they're spending far more time on.

**It realistically takes about one hour a week.**
That's the figure cited across sources, compared to the 19-20 hours a week the average entrepreneur spends on marketing overall. Expect a 30-60 day patience window before traffic noticeably picks up, with readable analytics after 1-2 months — and once it's working, it keeps compounding with very little upkeep. If it proves itself, outsourcing the whole weekly routine to a VA was costed at roughly $30-35/hour.

**Keyword research is the foundation everything else sits on.**
Two methods came up repeatedly: the *autocomplete method* (typing a keyword into search and noting what Pinterest suggests) and the *alphabet soup method* (typing a keyword plus each letter A-Z to surface longtail variations). Pinterest Trends is used to validate keyword volume and spot seasonal patterns — sources recommend planning content 45-60 days ahead of a peak. Board names should match Pinterest's official "interest" categories, and keywords should sit early in titles, descriptions, and blog posts. Underneath all of this is Pinterest's "annotation" system — hidden keyword tags the platform assigns based on your title, description, URL, board name, and even what its AI detects inside the image itself.

**Pins need to be genuinely different from each other, not just relabelled.**
Pinterest now detects "same-ish" pins — changing only a title or tweaking a design slightly no longer works. Each pin needs its own keywords, target situation, hook, visual, and board. The recommended approach: create three pins per product, each with a different photo and keyword angle, and stagger them roughly a week apart over three weeks rather than posting back to back. Multiple sources also stressed linking pins straight to the specific product/checkout page rather than a general shop page, since that reduces friction and improves conversion.

**Newer pins can rank faster than people assume — within 2-4 weeks**, especially for newer accounts, rather than the "months" older advice suggested. To rank well for a keyword, the recommended method is to study the top-ranking pins for that term (saves, how long they've been up, their annotations, style, colours, description wording) and then publish something more specific and better.

**Schedulers don't hurt your reach.** Testing referenced in the sources found no meaningful difference in distribution between posting live, using Pinterest's own scheduler, or using Tailwind. Tailwind's newer "Turbo Pin" feature was noted as encouraging reciprocal engagement that signals quality back to the algorithm.

**Three things separate accounts that work from ones that don't: consistency, real (not AI-generated) images, and proper keyword use.** Sources were specific that Pinterest's AI now reads and categorises images directly, so real product photos and real customer examples are favoured over generated visuals — and inconsistency was named as the most common mistake business owners make on the platform.
