## Keyword research — do this before making a single pin

<a name="the-keyword-research-process"></a>

### Free methods to find what people actually search for

**Autocomplete method**
Type your product into the Pinterest search bar and write down everything the dropdown suggests. Do this for every product line.
> Example: typing "branding kit" surfaces "branding kit for coaches," "branding kit Canva," "branding kit for photographers" — these are actual high-volume searches.

**Alphabet soup method**
Add each letter A-Z after your keyword one at a time ("branding kit a," "branding kit b"…) and record every suggestion. Aim for **20-30 longtail phrases per topic** before you start making anything.

### Choosing which phrases to target (PinClicks)

Use **PinClicks** to get scored data on your shortlist. The goal is to find phrases where:
- The score shows real search volume
- The current top-ranking pins are **old and non-specific** — that's your gap to rank in

> Real example from sources: "day hike lunch ideas" was rejected because newer pins were already flooding it. "Day hike first aid kit" was chosen because the top results were old, generic pins — a fresh, targeted pin could beat them quickly.

For each winning phrase, open the top-ranking pins and study them:
- How many saves do they have?
- What visual style do they use? (text on photo? flat graphic? lifestyle shot?)
- What words appear in every title?
- What colours dominate?

This tells you exactly what Pinterest already considers "winning" for that phrase — make something better.

### Finding the hidden tags that control distribution (Annotations)

Every pin has invisible keyword tags ("annotations") assigned by Pinterest using signals from the title, description, board name, URL, and **objects its AI detects in the image itself**. These tags determine which searches your pin shows up in.

**How to see them:** PinClicks shows the annotations on the top 25 ranking pins for any phrase, with how often each tag appears. Use the top 3-5 most common ones in your own title, description, and board name.

> Example from sources: the top-ranking "day hike first aid kit" pin carried these annotations: Camping Supplies, Camping And Hiking, Backpacking Tips, Hiking First Aid Kit, Hiking Essentials — all fed back into the new pin's copy.

### Naming boards correctly (Interest Explorer)

Board names must match **official Pinterest interest categories** — not phrases you invented.

Use **Interest Explorer** (free, built into Pinterest) to check: search your phrase. If nothing comes up, broaden the term until it hits. Use that exact wording for the board name.

> Example: searching "day hike first aid kit" returned nothing. Broadening to "first aid kit" surfaced "hiking first aid kit" and "hiking first aid kit checklist" as real categories. Those became the board names.

### Seasonal timing (Pinterest Trends)

Pinterest Trends is free and lives in your Pinterest account. To map a full season of content timing in one sitting:

1. Set "Trend type" to **Top monthly trends**
2. Choose your interest category
3. Set the end date to last July 31 — note what's trending
4. Move the date to Sept 30, Oct 31, Nov 30, Dec 31 — note what peaks when
5. Create those pins **45-60 days before the peak**, not the week before

> Example keywords surfaced: "thanksgiving table settings," "christmas decor ideas for living room" — pinned in October/November rather than December when everyone else is racing to catch up.

For Switzertemplates: "new business launch" spikes in January, "rebrand" spikes post-EOFY, "Instagram templates" has steady demand with a New Year bump.

## Two pin scheduling recipes — copy one of these exactly

<a name="two-copy-paste-pin-scheduling-recipes"></a>

A "fresh pin" = new image + new title + new description → same URL. No new product needed.

### Recipe 1 — Simple (good starting point)

1. Pick a product. Make **3 different pins** for it — different photo, different hook, different keywords each time.
2. Write copy by pasting your keywords into ChatGPT and asking for "SEO-driven Pinterest titles and descriptions" — then shorten the output (AI titles always run too long).
3. Schedule them **one per week over 3 weeks** — never two pins for the same product on consecutive days.
4. For 5 products: 15 pins → 5 weeks of steady coverage, batched in one session.

### Recipe 2 — Full recipe (better long-term coverage)

1. Take a product or blog post. Open **Pinterest Trends** on desktop (easier than mobile), search the keywords it's built around, set date range to 1 year.
2. Identify **3 keyword sets**: the original keywords + 2 more from the related suggestions Pinterest shows below the graph.
3. Make **2 pins per keyword set = 6 pins total**, all linking to the same URL. Each pin has different text overlay targeting its keyword set.
4. Set up **3 boards**: 1 existing board + 2 new boards named *exactly* after the other two keyword sets. Generate board descriptions by giving ChatGPT the target keywords and asking it to write an SEO-optimised description.
5. Schedule **one pin per week**, rotating boards: week 1 → board A, week 2 → board B, week 3 → board C, week 4 → back to board A.

**Result:** one product stays covered for **5-6 weeks** without touching it again.

> This is Megan's (recipe blogger) exact strategy. Her impressions and clicks reversed a full quarter of decline after she started using it. She attributes the change not to posting more, but to the consistency the rotation forces.

## What actually gets a pin clicked — not just saved

<a name="what-actually-gets-a-pin-clicked-not-just-saved"></a>

### Must-haves

**Text overlay on every pin**
99% of top-clicked pins (measured by outbound clicks, not saves) carry text on the image. Without it, pins get saved to mood boards but don't drive traffic.

**Outcome hook, not a feature list**
- ✗ "1,000+ Canva templates included" — gets saved
- ✓ "Look client-ready by Friday" — gets clicked

The outcome needs to be specific and slightly urgent. Vague benefit statements don't convert.

**Lifestyle mockups over flat product shots**
Pinterest's AI reads the actual image to categorise the pin — not just the text. Real-feeling photos (a template open on a laptop in a styled home office, a site on a phone in someone's hand) outperform screenshot grids and white-background product shots.

**Link to the product page, not the shop**
> One Etsy seller found that linking to her whole shop produced lots of favourites and zero sales. Switching to linking each pin directly to the individual product listing got those items into customers' Etsy carts — where they resurfaced every time that person returned to Etsy.

For Shopify: link to the **checkout page** directly, so the customer can pay immediately via Apple Pay or PayPal.

### Technical spec

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 2:3 |
| Dimensions | 1000 × 1500 px |
| Title (keyword placement) | First 30-40 characters (Pinterest cuts off on mobile) |
| Description hook | First 50-60 characters (shows before "read more") |
| Faces on pins | Underperform — except fashion, makeup, jewellery niches |

## Traffic stalled? 6 causes — each with a specific fix

<a name="when-traffic-stalls--the-6-actual-causes-and-fixes"></a>

> One account applied this framework and went from nothing to **180,000 outbound clicks in 90 days** on a beauty-niche account.

The core principle: **stalled traffic is a signals problem, not a design problem.** Pinterest's algorithm needs clear signals to know who you are and who to show you to. These six things break that signal.

| Cause | What it actually means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough data | Account too new or posting too rarely — nothing to distribute | Pin more, systematically |
| Inconsistent posting | Posting 20 one week, 2 the next confuses categorisation | Fixed schedule every week — even if it's just 5 pins |
| Flat titles | No curiosity gap — reader already knows what you're about to tell them | Rewrite around what they *want* to know, not what you have |
| Traffic leak | Visitors bounce within ~10 seconds → Pinterest sees that and penalises your reach | Stronger opening hooks on the landing page + related-product links to keep people moving |
| Guessing keywords | Intuition instead of data = pins that answer questions nobody is asking | PinClicks, Pinterest Trends, the methods above |
| No feedback loop | Never checking what works = repeating the same mistakes | Regular analytics review — double down on what's already performing |

## Tools — what they actually do

<a name="tools"></a>

**PinClicks** (paid)
Keyword research. Returns scored phrases with competition data. Also shows the hidden annotation tags on the top 25 ranking pins for any phrase — i.e. the exact categories Pinterest has decided those pins belong to. Use the top 3-5 annotations directly in your own pin copy.
→ pinclicks.com

**Pinterest Trends** (free — inside Pinterest)
Search volume graphs across any time period. Use the month-by-month scanning method (move the end date back through the year) to map a full seasonal content calendar in 20 minutes.

**Tailwind** (paid scheduler)
One Pinterest manager tested Tailwind vs. Pinterest's native scheduler vs. manually posting live every day for 6 years — no meaningful difference in reach between any of them. Use whichever creates less friction.
- **Free keyword tool**: paste a keyword or URL, get related terms + a "resonance" score (useful when Pinterest won't show actual volume figures)
- **Turbo Pin** (beta): engage with 10 other creators' pins first to unlock a submission to its shared feed. One tester's single submission (a pin she already believed would do well) became her best-performing pin of the month: 521 impressions, 11 engagements, 1 save.

**Pin Generator** (paid)
Paste a product link or upload an image → 10-20 pin variations in minutes, ready to schedule. Useful for hitting a consistent daily floor (10-12 pins/day cited as a minimum in one source) without designing each one manually.

**Interest Explorer** (free — inside Pinterest)
Find the official Pinterest interest category names for your topic. These are the correct words to use for board names and pin descriptions.

## When the algorithm changes — the exact 3-step protocol

<a name="algorithm-changes"></a>

From a creator who built a six-figure blog income through Pinterest over 6 years:

1. **Wait a week.** 90% of drops self-correct without any action.
2. **If still down after a week: change exactly one thing** — not your whole strategy — and give it **3 weeks** to show a result.
3. **Never overhaul everything at once.** You'll lose the ability to tell what was actually working.

Additional advice from the same source:
- **Niche clarity speeds growth.** An account Pinterest can clearly read as "for coaches and service providers" grows faster than one it reads as "general small business stuff." It's the difference between a dog account and an animals account.
- Mix **Canva-made pins** with **repurposed video** (e.g. from TikTok) — Pinterest cycles through phases of favouring one format over the other, so having both keeps you covered.
- Even after deliberately scaling back her pinning, this creator's monthly page views from Pinterest "barely changed" — still hundreds of thousands a month.
