## 1. Subject Line Formulas

### The "Banger Offer" Formula
Maxwell emphasizes that "gone are the days of just doing 10% off" — your subject line needs to signal real value immediately.

**Templates:**
- `[Specific benefit] + [timeframe]` → "New client-ready templates in 10 minutes"
- `The [thing they want] without [thing they hate]` → "A polished brand without the designer price tag"
- `[Number] [desirable thing] + [qualifier]` → "7 website sections ready to customize tonight"
- `What [aspirational peer] taught me about [their goal]` → "What a 6-figure coach taught me about first impressions"

**Examples for Switzertemplates:**
- "Your next client is judging your website right now"
- "I rebuilt this Wix page in 12 minutes (here's how)"
- "The template bundle I'd buy if I were starting over"
- "Stop paying designers for something you can do yourself"

### The Pattern Interrupt
Maxwell's content consistently breaks expected patterns. For a warm list with 15% opens, surprise them:

- "I almost didn't send this one"
- "This isn't a sales email (okay, maybe a little)"
- "The template I use for my own business"
- "You bought from me 6 months ago — here's what's changed"

## 2. Opening Hooks for Warm Lists

Your subscribers already trust you. They've bought. The hook isn't about credibility — it's about **relevance and curiosity**.

### The "I Did The Work" Hook
Maxwell opens by establishing he's done the research so they don't have to:

> "I spent the last few days scrolling social media to see what kind of advice is being shared today... Honestly, there's a mix of everything."

**Adapt for Switzertemplates:**
> "I've been looking at what small business owners are posting on Instagram this week — and I noticed something. Most of you are overthinking your brand visuals."

> "Last Tuesday, I helped three customers customize the same template. Each one looked completely different. Here's what they did."

### The "Specificity = Believability" Hook
Maxwell frequently uses exact numbers to build trust:

> "I put it through hundreds of emails at this point, and the results actually surprise me."

**Adapt for Switzertemplates:**
> "I've watched 43 customers use this exact Canva template this month. The ones who sold out their services did one thing differently."

> "This template has been downloaded 2,847 times. But only about 30% of people use it the way it's designed. Here's what the top sellers do instead."

### The "Behind The Curtain" Hook
For warm audiences, showing your process builds connection:

> "So, in this video, I'm not only going to show you exactly how it works and walk you through the exact workflow. I'm going to be actually using this process to create [X] live directly in front of you."

**Adapt for Switzertemplates:**
> "I'm going to show you exactly how I'd customize this template if I were launching a coaching business tomorrow — step by step, no fluff."

## 3. Body Copy Structure

### The Maxwell Flow: Context → Proof → Walkthrough → Offer

**Step 1: Context (Why this matters now)**
Maxwell always frames why the reader should care *today*:

> "For people looking to create a side hustle and build a business online, there is nothing better than..."

Your version:
> "If you've been putting off updating your website because it feels overwhelming, this is the easiest place to start."

**Step 2: Proof (Show don't tell)**
He uses specific results and examples, not vague claims:

> "We had an eight-figure brand come in, 1.8 mil a month, and then after we doubled the email, they were doing 240k the next month."

Your version:
> "Sarah downloaded this template on a Thursday night. By Saturday, she'd booked two discovery calls from her new website. Her exact words: 'I didn't know it could be this fast.'"

**Step 3: Walkthrough (Make it feel doable)**
Maxwell breaks things into numbered steps or components:

> "First, we're going to start with the setup and the offers... So, the next 15 minutes, I'm going to be breaking down this record-breaking funnel for you."

Your version:
> "Here's what's inside:
> - 1 homepage template (the exact layout that converts browsers into buyers)
> - 3 service page variations (pick the one that fits your offer)
> - The font + color pairings already done for you"

**Step 4: Offer (Remove the risk)**
Maxwell emphasizes making it "so stupid for the customer not to buy":

> "Make it so stupid for the customer not to buy from you."

Your version:
> "Grab the bundle today, and if you can't figure out how to customize it in 30 minutes, I'll send you a video walkthrough personally."

## 4. CTA Techniques That Convert

### Make the CTA the Obvious Next Step
Maxwell's emails and funnels always have one clear action. Never multiple competing CTAs.

**Weak CTA:**
> "Check out our shop for new templates!"

**Strong CTA:**
> "→ See the exact template Sarah used to book 2 clients this week"

### Use "See" and "Get" Over "Buy" or "Shop"
For warm lists, reduce friction language:

- "See what's inside →"
- "Get the template →"
- "Grab your copy →"
- "Start customizing →"

### The "Finish Their Sentence" CTA
Position the CTA as the answer to what they're already thinking:

> "If you've been staring at your website wondering where to start — **this is the answer →**"

> "Ready to stop tweaking and start launching? **Get the bundle here →**"

### Stack a Soft CTA Before the Hard CTA
For longer emails, give them an early exit:

> "If you're already sold, **here's the link →**"
> [Continue with more detail for those who need convincing]
> "Still here? Good. Let me show you exactly what you get..."

## 5. Re-engagement Tactics

Your list has 21,000 past buyers — they're warm, but quiet. Don't guilt-trip. **Re-earn their attention.**

### Tactic 1: The "Here's What's Changed" Email
Maxwell frequently references evolution and improvement. Use it:

**Subject:** "A lot has changed since you last bought"

**Body:**
> "You grabbed [product] a while back — and since then, I've added 47 new templates to the shop.
>
> I'm not going to flood your inbox. But I thought you'd want to know what's new — especially if you're working on [specific project like launching a new offer, refreshing your brand, etc.].
>
> Here are the 3 newest templates that are getting the most love right now:
> [Link 1]
> [Link 2]
> [Link 3]
>
> See anything that fits? Hit reply and I'll tell you which one I'd pick for your business."

### Tactic 2: The "Honest Check-In" Email
**Subject:** "Still there?"

**Body:**
> "I noticed you haven't opened an email in a while — and I get it. Inboxes are chaos.
>
> If Switzertemplates isn't useful to you anymore, no hard feelings. You can unsubscribe below.
>
> But if you're still building your business and want templates that actually save you time — **I'll keep showing up.**
>
> Here's something worth opening for: [link to high-value blog post or freebie]"

### Tactic 3: The "Best Of" Roundup
For subscribers who've gone cold, curate your best content so they can self-select back in:

**Subject:** "The 5 most-downloaded templates this year"

**Body:**
> "In case you missed it, here's what your fellow business owners are loving right now:
>
> 1. [Template name] — perfect for [use case]
> 2. [Template name] — best for [use case]
> ...
>
> Curious which one fits your business? Hit reply and I'll give you my honest take."

## 6. What to Avoid

### ❌ Firing Pop-Ups (or Emails) Too Aggressively
Maxwell critiques: *"This pop-up fired almost immediately. I can tell you right now this is underperforming."*

**For emails:** Don't send 5 emails in a week after months of silence. Ease back in with 1 high-value email, wait, then build frequency.

### ❌ Too Much Text, No Visual Hierarchy
Maxwell notes: *"There's a ton of text right here... It's just not an optimized form."*

**For emails:**
- Use short paragraphs (1-2 sentences max)
- Use line breaks generously
- Bold the key benefit so skimmers catch it
- One CTA button, not three

### ❌ Generic Offers
Maxwell: *"Gone are the days of just doing 10% off. That is just not what works."*

Instead of "10% off your next purchase," try:
- "Free bonus template when you grab the bundle this week"
- "Buy 2 templates, get 1 free (your pick)"
- "First 20 buyers get a 15-min customization call with me"

### ❌ Vague or Exaggerated Claims
Maxwell always uses specific numbers and real examples. Avoid:
- "This will change everything!" → Say what it actually changes
- "Hundreds of happy customers" → "Downloaded by 2,341 business owners"
- "Amazing results" → "Sarah booked 2 calls in 48 hours"

### ❌ Competing CTAs
Every email should have **one job**. If you're promoting a blog post, don't also push a product and ask them to follow you on Instagram. Pick one.
