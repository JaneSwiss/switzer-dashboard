Pinterest
Keyword research — do this before making a single pin
  • Autocomplete — type your product into the Pinterest search bar, write down every dropdown suggestion. "Branding kit" surfaces "branding kit for coaches," "branding kit Canva," "branding kit for photographers."
  • Alphabet soup — add A-Z after your keyword one letter at a time ("branding kit a," "branding kit b"…). Reliably uncovers 20-30 longtail phrases you'd never think of.
  • PinClicks — shows which phrases are saturated vs. winnable. Target phrases where the top-ranking pins are old and non-specific — that's your gap. Example: sources rejected "day hike lunch ideas" (too many new pins targeting it already) and chose "day hike first aid kit" instead because the current top results were old and generic.
  • Name boards after real search phrases — check the official Pinterest interest category names in Interest Explorer (broaden your term if nothing comes up) and use those exact words, not invented ones.

One product → 15-20 different pins, no new content needed

A "fresh pin" is a new image + new title + new description pointing at the same URL. Two recipes to choose from:

  • Simple: 3 pins per product, each with a different photo and keyword angle. Schedule one per week across 3 weeks — never two for the same product on consecutive days (they compete with each other).
  • Full recipe: 6 pins per product built around 3 keyword sets (2 pins each). Post to 1 existing board + 2 new boards named after the keyword sets. Rotate A→B→C→A, one pin per week. One product stays covered for 5-6 weeks without touching it again.
→ both recipes step by step
What actually gets a pin clicked — not just saved
  • Text overlay on the image — 99% of top-clicked pins have it. Without text, pins get saved to mood boards but don't drive traffic.
  • Outcome hook, not a feature list — "look client-ready by Friday" gets clicked; "1,000+ Canva templates included" gets saved, not clicked. Be specific and slightly urgent.
  • Lifestyle mockups over flat product shots — Pinterest's AI reads the actual image to categorise it. A template on a laptop in a styled home office outperforms a screenshot grid on a white background.
  • Link to the product page, not the shop — one Etsy seller found shop-page links produced favourites but zero sales. Product-page links put items in customers' Etsy carts, where they resurface on every return visit.

Traffic stalled? 6 causes — each with a specific fix

Stalled traffic is a signals problem, not a design problem. One account applied these fixes and hit 180,000 outbound clicks in 90 days:

  • Account too new / too low-volume → pin more, systematically
  • Inconsistent posting → fixed schedule beats high volume every time
  • Flat titles (no curiosity gap) → rewrite around what the reader wants to know, not what you have
  • High bounce within 10 seconds → stronger opening hooks on the landing page + related links to keep people moving
  • Guessing at keywords → use PinClicks and Pinterest Trends with real data
  • No feedback loop → review analytics, double down on what's already working

AI
What AI is actually changing — and what it can't replace
  • Token economics — replacing a full year of knowledge work costs ~$200K in AI tokens, and that cost is falling. Anyone working at a computer is now in direct competition with AI's cost to do the same job.
  • Five things people will still be paid for — conviction (AI is sycophantic and has none), judgment (experience-based decisions), taste (creative choices that match an ideal customer), commercial outcomes (attributed sales), and improving the system itself.
  • 82% of people haven't used AI yet — the window to get ahead of this is still open. Unlike Amazon FBA or Etsy, the space isn't saturated. Almost no one outside tech circles has heard of Claude Code.
  • Personal brand is the competitive moat — 86% of content is predicted to be AI-generated avatars. The people who are known, liked, and trusted in their niche will win. Authentic human identity is what AI cannot replicate.
